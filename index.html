CDRL Submission and Tracking Workflow

A controlled intake and tracking system built around the complete deliverable lifecycle, from guided submission and report-number assignment through attachment processing, status visibility, and administrator recovery.

Problem

A manual process made identifier control, metadata quality, attachments, status, and failure recovery difficult to manage consistently.

My Role

Mapped the lifecycle, designed and built the workflow, tested each path, and wrote contributor and administrator guidance.

Result

One guided interface, a centralized register, sequential report identifiers, organized files, meaningful notifications, and traceable recovery.

View Documentation Decisions

  • Tell users what happened and whether they must correct, verify, wait, or resubmit.
  • Give administrators the failure point and concrete recovery steps.
  • Preserve valid work instead of forcing an unnecessary restart.

Role-Based Onboarding and Offboarding

Six coordinated SOPs containing more than 230 actions for civilian, contractor, and military personnel, including entry-condition branches, prerequisites, dependencies, concurrent work, responsible offices, and completion evidence.

Why Six Guides Instead of One

A universal SOP would have forced each reader to interpret irrelevant conditions. Separating personnel categories and process direction created shorter accurate paths while preserving shared structure and terminology. The suite was selected for division-wide adoption.

Information Architecture and Governance

A 60-plus-page operating guide defining repository structures, taxonomy, navigation, naming conventions, permissions, lifecycle practices, and stewardship across three classified and unclassified environments.

Working Method

Investigate Before Explaining

I do not treat documentation as a polish layer added after the system is complete. Documentation helps expose missing decisions, inconsistent behavior, unclear ownership, and support risk.

  1. FrameDefine the audience, decision, workflow, constraints, and evidence of completion.
  2. InvestigateUse the system, interview stakeholders, compare requirements, and identify failure states.
  3. StructureSeparate concepts, procedures, references, roles, pathways, and exceptions.
  4. TestValidate instructions against observed behavior and revise where readers lose the path.
  5. MaintainMake ownership, revision history, standards, and lifecycle expectations visible.
