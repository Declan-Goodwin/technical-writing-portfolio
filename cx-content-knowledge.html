Information Architecture and Governance

A content-governance model for large repositories spread across multiple technical environments, designed to make information easier to find, understand, own, and maintain.

Problem

Inconsistent naming, navigation, ownership, permissions, and lifecycle practices made retrieval depend too heavily on individual memory.

My Role

Designed the information architecture, authored a 60+ page governance SOP, and created diagrams and quick-reference derivatives.

Result

A shared operating model for content structure and stewardship across three environments, with clearer search paths and maintenance expectations.

View Content Decisions
  • Organize information around enduring work rather than individual preference.
  • Use predictable content patterns so users can infer where information belongs.
  • Make ownership and stewardship explicit instead of leaving responsibility implicit.
  • Test labels and navigation with users and revise where they lose the path.
  • Convert dense governance material into progressive layers of guidance for high-frequency tasks.

Role-Based Onboarding and Offboarding

Six coordinated guides containing more than 230 actions for civilian, contractor, and military personnel, structured around role, process direction, dependencies, concurrent work, and completion evidence.

Why Separate the Content

A universal checklist would have forced each reader to interpret irrelevant conditions. Splitting guidance by personnel category and process direction created shorter accurate paths while preserving common structure, terminology, and ownership. The suite was selected for division-wide adoption.

Content Method

Reduce Uncertainty Before Adding More Words.

Good support and knowledge content should make the system feel smaller. The reader should be able to locate the right information, understand the current state, and identify the next useful action without translating the organization in their head.

  1. FindMake the right content discoverable through structure, terminology, taxonomy, and predictable navigation.
  2. OrientTell the reader where they are in the process and what changed.
  3. ActGive a concrete next step and remove instructions that do not apply to this user or state.
  4. RecoverDocument exceptions, ownership, and recovery paths so users do not have to improvise when the happy path breaks.
  5. MaintainMake ownership, review expectations, terminology, and lifecycle rules visible so useful content stays useful.
